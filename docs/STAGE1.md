# Stage 1: the RAM-responder board

**Goal (from the hand-off):** prove the hard part of the whole project, a Tang
Nano 20K answering the PC Engine's bus through the rear expansion port, before
any CD logic exists. The board makes the console see RAM at banks `$80–$87`,
where the CD-ROM² work RAM lives. A memory test run from a HuCard then writes
and reads it.

**Why first:** it exercises everything the CD board will need (the connector,
5 V ↔ 3.3 V translation, address decoding, read timing, bus contention) with
the fewest parts. If it fails, it fails cheaply.

Status: **design, not ordered.** Every claim below says where it comes from.

---

## 1. The port

The rear port is a **3×23, 0.1" pin header** on the PC Engine, CoreGrafx and
CoreGrafx II (not the Shuttle). Our board needs a matching 3×23 **female**
header.

The full pinout is in [`ext_bus_pinout.csv`](ext_bus_pinout.csv), generated by
[`tools/ext_bus.py`](../tools/ext_bus.py). That script checks two independent
sources against each other, and **they agree on all 69 pins**:

| source | what it is |
|---|---|
| [`PC_Engine_Schematics_r1.2.pdf`](datasheets/PC_Engine_Schematics_r1.2.pdf), sheet 5, CN102 | KiCad redraw of the console, reverse-engineered from the board. Read from 400 dpi crops in [`datasheets/evidence/`](datasheets/evidence/) |
| [PCEdev wiki, EXT BUS pinout](datasheets/pcedev-wiki-EXT_BUS_pinout-2026-11-19.txt) | community pinout, with the physical orientation |

**Orientation** (wiki): looking into the back of the console, AV/RF port up,
power jack down, card slot away from you. The wiki's left/middle/right columns
are rows a/b/c; its table rows are pins 1–23.

> **Gate before ordering:** neither source is a measurement. Check continuity on
> the real console: a handful of pins (A0, D0, /RD, GND, +5A) with a multimeter
> against the CPU's pins, using the same orientation.

What the port gives a stage-1 board:

| needed | on the port |
|---|---|
| address | A0–A20, all 21 |
| data | D0–D7 |
| strobes | /RD, /WR |
| reset | /RESET |
| ground | GND on c8, c17, c20 (digital). GNDA on a21 and c2 is the **analog** ground, not a return path |
| power | **none usable.** +5A is the analog supply for the video chip; EXTPOW is ~9 V going **into** the console from a CD unit |

## 2. The Tang Nano 20K's pins

All 34 header I/Os are in [`tang_nano_20k_header.csv`](tang_nano_20k_header.csv),
transcribed from the Sipeed datasheet's pinout page
([evidence](datasheets/evidence/tang-nano-20k-pinout.png)). Every header bank is
3.3 V (datasheet legend), so none of them can touch the 5 V bus directly.

| status | count | pins |
|---|---|---|
| free (display connector only, nothing fitted) | 12 | 77, 27, 28, 25, 26, 29, 30, 31, 42, 41, 48, 49 |
| usable (shared with an on-board LED, the speaker amp's inputs, or HDMI's EDID lines) | 12 | 15–20, 51, 54, 55, 56, 52, 53 |
| **unverified** (BL616 lines FPGA-Companion doesn't use) | 4 | 71, 72, 73, 74 |
| kept for later (microSD, the companion link, the RGB LED) | 6 | 75, 76, 80, 85, 86, 79 |

**24 pins are safe today.** The 4 BL616 lines need a test first: in PCEHeroTN,
BL616 wiring was part of a hard-to-pin-down crash (#189).

## 3. Pin budget

| signal | pins | direction (FPGA's view) |
|---|---|---|
| A0–A12 (8 KB window) | 13 | in |
| A13–A15 (rest of 64 KB) | 3 | in |
| D0–D7 | 8 | both |
| /RD, /WR | 2 | in |
| /SEL (our decoded bank select) | 1 | in |
| **total for 64 KB** | **27** | |
| **total for 8 KB** | **24** | |

**64 KB needs 27 pins, three more than the 24 safe ones.** So:

- **The board wires the full 64 KB.** A13–A15 go to three of the BL616 lines
  (71, 72, 73).
- **The first firmware uses 8 KB at bank `$80`** and ignores those three pins.
  That's enough to prove the bus.
- **After a test shows the BL616 leaves those lines alone,** the firmware
  enables all 64 KB. Same board, no respin either way.

Two signals the FPGA doesn't drive, to save pins (see §4): the data translator's
**direction** and **output enable** come from the bus and the decoder.
/RESET isn't needed for a RAM responder.

## 4. Design decisions

| # | decision | why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Address decode outside the FPGA.** One 8-bit comparator (74HCT688, on the bus's 5 V side) matches A20–A16 = `1 0000` and outputs /SEL | banks `$80–$87` all share A20–A16; saves 5 FPGA pins. These banks are where the CD-ROM² unit's own RAM lives, so a console without one leaves them empty. A HuCard can't answer there either: its /CE (slot pin 24) **is** the CPU's A20, so a card is only selected when A20 = 0 ([HuCard pinout](datasheets/pcedev-wiki-HuCard_pinout-2026-11-19.txt)) |
| 2 | **Data bus through a dual-supply translator, 74LVC8T245**, its **A side (VCCA = 5 V) facing the console** and B side (3.3 V) the FPGA, **not** a 74LVC245 | a 74LVC245 at 3.3 V would drive only ~3.3 V highs onto a 5 V bus. The HuC6280 manual we have gives **no input thresholds**; if its inputs are CMOS-level (often 0.7 × 5 V = 3.5 V), 3.3 V is not a reliable high. The 8T245 drives full 5 V levels both ways |
| 3 | **Address and strobe inputs through 74LVC245A at 3.3 V**, fixed direction bus → FPGA | its inputs tolerate 5 V; one supply; 3 chips cover 20 lines |
| 4 | **Translator direction = /RD, enable = /SEL**, straight from the bus | the 8T245's DIR and /OE are referenced to VCCA, so with the 5 V side on A the console's /RD and the decoder's 5 V output drive them directly, no inverter: /RD high = A→B (bus to FPGA), /RD low = B→A (FPGA drives the bus). The FPGA can only drive the bus during a read of our banks. Saves 2 FPGA pins. Small series resistors (33 Ω) on the FPGA-side data lines limit current during the few ns when direction flips |
| 5 | **Bus-side 5 V comes from the console's +5A**, the Tang runs from its own USB-C | when the console is off, everything touching its bus is unpowered and can't back-feed it. The 8T245 disables its outputs when either supply is off. **Open:** +5A is the analog video supply; our draw is small, but watch for video noise |
| 6 | **No connection between the Tang's USB 5 V and the console** | two supplies, one shared ground |
| 7 | **The FPGA's data pins stay inputs until configured**, and the data translator is enabled only by /SEL | an unconfigured FPGA can't drive the bus |

## 5. Open questions, each with how it gets answered

| question | answered by |
|---|---|
| Does the real port match the two sources? | multimeter continuity on the console (gate before ordering) |
| HuC6280 input thresholds (is 3.3 V a high?) | moot with decision 2; a scope on a real console would settle it |
| Read timing: when must data be valid after /RD falls, at 7.16 MHz? | the manual we have has **no AC timing**. Measure /RD and the console's own RAM data on a scope, or find the HuC6280 datasheet's timing tables. Translators add ~5 ns each way; FPGA block RAM is single-cycle |
| Do the BL616 lines 71–73 stay quiet? | build a tiny FPGA image that only reads them, with FPGA-Companion running, and log their levels |
| Does drawing from +5A disturb the video? | look at the picture with the board fitted |
| Where does the 3×23 female header come from? | any 0.1" 3-row female header; check the console's pin length and the shroud clearance |

## 6. The schematic

[`hw/stage1/pce_exp_stage1.kicad_sch`](../hw/stage1/pce_exp_stage1.kicad_sch) is **generated** from
[`hw/stage1/design.py`](../hw/stage1/design.py) by `python tools/gen_stage1.py`. Edit the data, not
the schematic. 33 parts: the port (J1), the Tang headers (J2, J3), the decoder (U1), the data
translator (U2), three input translators (U3–U5), 8 series resistors, 8 capacitors, 6 test points.

`python tools/check_stage1.py --selftest` asks **KiCad itself** for the netlist and checks it:
every pin on its designed net, the rules re-derived from the CSVs (port pins, Tang pins we may use,
supplies, translator directions, the decoder, 5 V never wired to the FPGA, FPGA nets point-to-point),
and KiCad's ERC. Its self-test breaks the netlist nine ways and must catch each. On its first run it
caught a real generator bug: resistors placed 7.62 mm apart touched pins and merged eight data nets.

## 7. The FPGA image, in simulation

[`rtl/ram_responder.v`](../rtl/ram_responder.v): every console input goes through one
two-stage synchroniser on a 108 MHz clock; RAM is read continuously so data waits
before /RD falls; the data pins are driven only while /RD and /SEL are low
(combinational); a write commits on /WR's rising edge. `ADDR_BITS` = 13 (8 KB, bank
`$80`) for the first image, 16 (64 KB) after test T5.

`sh sim/run_ram_responder.sh` runs it on a model of the board (decoder, translators,
their datasheet delays) and a 7.16 MHz bus that's asynchronous to the FPGA clock.

| run | result |
|---|---|
| 8 KB and 64 KB, nominal bus (address setup 20 ns, 60 ns strobe) | **PASS**: 0 data errors over ~10–17k checked reads, no FPGA/translator fight longer than 7 ns, 20 ns read margin |
| three broken copies (drives during writes; never commits a write; writes on any address) | **all FAIL**, as they must |
| **sweep:** how soon after the address changes can the console sample read data? | **fails at ≤ 50 ns, clean at 55 ns** |

**What that means for T3:** our data settles on the bus ~50 ns after the address
changes. Most of that is the 74HCT688 decode (35 ns, datasheet worst case) and the
translator (8 ns). The console's cycle is 140 ns; if T3 shows it samples read data
later than ~55 ns into the cycle (expected: near its end), we have margin. If not,
the fix is known: a faster decoder (74AHCT family), or decode in the FPGA.

Not covered by the simulation: the real HuC6280 pin timing (T3), metastability,
the Gowin PLL, analog effects.

## 8. What happens next

1. **Schematic review** (you, in KiCad): open `hw/stage1/pce_exp_stage1.kicad_pro`.
   The Tang is one part (A1) with one footprint, so its two header rows are fixed
   20.32 mm apart: measured from the MiSTeryShield20k RPi Pico's TangNano-20K
   footprint and the Sipeed drawing, which also confirmed all 34 I/O positions.
2. **Placement** (you): the PCB already has every footprint and net, staged
   beside the board (KiCad schematic parity: 0 differences). See
   [PLACEMENT_BRIEF.md](PLACEMENT_BRIEF.md). Built by
   `tools/make_stage1_pcb.py` (run it with KiCad's Python), which also writes the
   design rules, the project rules and `board.json`; PCB HERO's
   `scripts/check_board.py hw/stage1 --selftest` passes.
3. **Routing** via PCB HERO, after placement.
4. **The FPGA image**: a RAM responder with a test HuCard program.
